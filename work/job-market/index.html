Job Market & Skills Analysis

What a focused sample of analyst, product and BI postings says about roles, experience and skills.

01 / The question

What are these jobs actually asking for?

Analyst, product and BI titles overlap, but they do not necessarily describe the same entry points or skill requirements. This analysis looks at role volume, experience labels, salaries and tool mentions within a defined job-posting sample.

14,681source postings
188target-role postings

Define the sample before comparing it.

A conservative, rule-based title classifier identifies Business Analyst, Product Management, Data Analyst, Business Intelligence and Product Analyst postings. Only two Product Analyst postings qualify, so that group cannot support a broad claim.

02 / My contribution

A reproducible analysis, from tables to findings.

Built the notebook workflow, relational schema and example SQL queries, joined job-to-skill and job-to-industry tables, and presented the findings visually. The notebook uses pandas joins; the SQL files document the relational structure and query approach. Missing labels are retained, and salary filtering is limited to the salary analysis.

The experience mix changes the comparison.

28 of the 188 target postings are labelled entry level (14.9%). Data Analyst has the largest entry-level share: 19 of 45 postings (42.2%). Experience labels are missing for 48 postings, so this is a description of this sample, not the entire hiring market.

03 / Analytical decisions

Keep different kinds of evidence separate.

Tags ≠ keyword mentions

Relational skill categories and words found in descriptions answer different questions. Multi-valued associations are not distinct job counts.

Salary needs its denominator

Salary medians use only usable normalized values. Different seniority mixes and small groups prevent a clean role-to-role pay conclusion.

Descriptive, not representative

Rule-based titles and literal keyword matching miss some meanings. The sample is a useful lens, not a census of demand.
